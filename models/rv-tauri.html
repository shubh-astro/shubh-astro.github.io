RV Tauri
Pulsator

RV Tauri variables are luminous pulsating stars whose light alternates between deep and shallow minima, running 30 to 150 days from one deep minimum to the next and changing by as much as four magnitudes. They are thought to be low-mass post-AGB stars, mostly binaries wrapped in a dusty disc. — Wikipedia. At IUCAA I studied one of these in NGC 6441, an unusually metal-rich globular cluster in the Galactic bulge, measuring its physical and chemical properties from high-resolution SALT spectra — the first high-resolution spectroscopic study of a variable star in that cluster. The model shows one breathing: the photosphere swelling and cooling toward minimum, shock fronts running out through the atmosphere, all of it inside the dust its own mass loss has built. Photosphere

Extended atmosphere

A loosely bound ochre envelope that lags the photosphere as it breathes.

Shock shells

Two at once: one rising through the atmosphere, one fading as it reaches the top.

Dust shell

Circumstellar dust, built by the star's own mass loss over many cycles.
